Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit our original research article titled **"Multi-Modal Machine Learning Framework for State-Level Dengue Outbreak Prediction in India: Integrating Climate Dynamics with Socio-Economic Vulnerability"** for consideration in the *Indian Journal of Medical Research*.

Dengue continues to escalate as a major public health challenge in India. While the National Vector Borne Disease Control Programme (NVBDCP) has made strides in surveillance, the system remains largely reactive. Our study addresses this gap by developing a robust, predictive machine learning framework ($R^2=0.888$) that forecasts outbreaks weeks in advance. 

**Key Novelty:**
Unlike traditional models that rely solely on climate data, our framework integrates structural vulnerability metrics—specifically the **NITI Aayog Health Index** and **GDP Per Capita**—to contextualize outbreak risk. We demonstrate that combining "Exposure" (climate/vector) with "Vulnerability" (health systems) significantly enhances predictive accuracy.

**Relevance to IJMR:**
We explicitly discuss the potential to replicate the success of the National TB Elimination Programme’s **Nikshay** ecosystem for vector-borne diseases. Given IJMR’s focus on translatable public health research relevant to national priorities, we believe this work will be of significant interest to your readership, including epidemiologists, policy makers, and program officers.

This manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration for publication elsewhere. I have no conflicts of interest to disclose.
